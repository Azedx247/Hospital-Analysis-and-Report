# Hospital-Analysis-and-Report

Hospital Admissions Dashboard — Data Analysis Report
Dataset: 1,000 patient records
Overall Readmission Rate: 21.0%
Overall Recovery Rate: 69.8%
Average Treatment Cost: 332,278
Average Length of Stay: 7.46 days
1. Key Findings
Highest Monthly Readmission Rate
October recorded the highest readmission rate at 26.74%, while August had the lowest at 14.29%.
Insight: October's higher rate suggests a need to review patient volume, case mix, discharge procedures, and follow-up practices during the month.

Department with the Most Readmissions
Cardiology recorded the highest number of readmissions with 38 cases and a 27.74% readmission rate. It accounted for 18.10% of all readmissions.
Insight: Cardiology should be a priority for reviewing discharge planning and post-discharge care.
Length of Stay and Readmission
Patients who stayed 1–3 days had the highest readmission rate at 31.58%, compared with 14.55% for patients staying 4–7 days.
Insight: Very short stays are associated with higher readmissions. Early discharge cases should therefore be reviewed, especially where follow-up may have been insufficient.
Highest-Risk Age Group
Patients aged 65–90 had the highest readmission rate at 36.39%. Although they represented 30.5% of patients, they accounted for approximately 52.9% of all readmissions.
Insight: Older patients should receive closer discharge planning, medication reviews, and post-discharge follow-up.
Treatment Cost and Patient Outcomes
Higher treatment costs did not show a significant improvement in recovery. Patients in the highest cost group had an average cost of 536,125 and a recovery rate of 70.4%, similar to the lowest-cost group.
Insight: Higher spending alone does not appear to guarantee better outcomes. Cost should be assessed alongside treatment effectiveness and patient outcomes.
High-Cost Departments with Poorer Outcomes
Cardiology and Oncology require closer attention.
•	Cardiology: Average cost of 378,818, recovery rate of 66.42%, and readmission rate of 27.74%.
•	Oncology: Average cost of 526,870, recovery rate of 68.29%, and readmission rate of 24.39%.
•	ICU: Highest average cost at 627,743, but recovery was relatively strong at 76.47%, so the main concern is cost efficiency rather than poor outcomes.

3. Management Summary
The analysis shows a 21.0% overall readmission rate with noticeable differences across departments, age groups, length of stay, and treatment costs.
The major areas of concern are Cardiology, patients aged 65–90, very short hospital stays, and high treatment costs without significant improvement in recovery.

Recommendations
1.	Strengthen discharge planning for patients with 1–3 day stays.
2.	Provide enhanced follow-up for patients aged 65+.
3.	Review the causes of high readmissions in Cardiology.
4.	Assess Oncology treatment costs against patient outcomes.
5.	Investigate the high readmission rate recorded in October.
6.	Monitor treatment costs alongside recovery and readmission rates.
7.	Continue tracking key KPIs such as readmission rate, recovery rate, treatment cost, and length of stay.
8.	
Conclusion
The analysis suggests that reducing readmissions should focus on better discharge planning, targeted follow-up, identifying high-risk patients, and improving departmental performance rather than simply increasing treatment spending or hospital stay duration.
